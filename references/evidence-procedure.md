# Evidence and procedure interface

Civil Code rights are often decided by evidence and procedure. Load this file when a dispute may move beyond informal advice.

## Evidence map categories
- Identity/relationship: ID, corporate registration, marriage/kinship, agency/authorization.
- Documents: contracts, receipts, IOUs, agreements, notices, property certificates, registrations.
- Payment: bank statements, payment-platform records, invoices, reconciliation.
- Communications: full-context chat/email/SMS, call records, lawful recordings.
- Digital/publication: URLs, account IDs, timestamps, platform records, metadata where available.
- Physical/property: photos/video, inspection, repair records, appraisals.
- Injury/loss: medical records, invoices, disability/appraisal where applicable, income/loss evidence.
- Witness/authority: witnesses, police/administrative/court/notarial records.

## Preservation principles
Preserve originals and full context; avoid editing source files; export/back up important digital records; record how evidence was obtained; preserve devices/accounts when proportionate; use lawful notarization/evidence-preservation mechanisms when needed.

Never advise hacking, impersonation, unauthorized account/device access, secret installation of surveillance software, evidence fabrication, deletion, concealment or coercion.

## Procedure checklist
Verify current rules before giving deadline/forum conclusions:
- proper parties;
- court jurisdiction or valid arbitration agreement;
- limitation/exclusion periods;
- filing requirements;
- property/conduct/evidence preservation;
- burden of proof and special presumptions;
- appraisal/expert issues;
- service;
- appeal/retrial only where relevant;
- enforcement and asset clues.

## Practical note
A strong substantive right may still be difficult to enforce if the defendant cannot be identified/served, evidence is weak, assets are unavailable, or the wrong forum/remedy is chosen. State these separately from the merits.
