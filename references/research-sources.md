# Research sources and verification discipline

## Preferred authority hierarchy
Use current official sources wherever available. For material propositions, prefer the primary legal text over summaries.

Priority sources/categories:
1. National People's Congress / National Laws and Regulations Database and NPC official resources (`flk.npc.gov.cn`, `npc.gov.cn`).
2. Supreme People's Court official resources (`court.gov.cn`, People's Court Gazette and official judicial-interpretation/case releases).
3. State Council / Central Government (`gov.cn`) for regulations and official publication.
4. Relevant ministries/commissions and national authorities for subject-specific rules.
5. Provincial/municipal people's congresses, governments and courts for valid local rules/practice where applicable.

## Source discipline
- Search results/snippets are discovery tools, not the final authority.
- Open the underlying official text when available.
- Verify title, issuing body, promulgation/effective dates, amendment/repeal/status, and relevant article.
- Never invent an article number from memory when verification is possible.
- Quote sparingly; prefer accurate paraphrase plus article/provision identification.
- Distinguish Civil Code provisions from judicial interpretations, procedural law, administrative regulation, local rules, and cases.
- Secondary legal commentary may identify issues but should not replace an available official source for a material conclusion.

## Civil Code baseline
The Civil Code of the People's Republic of China was adopted in 2020 and came into force on 2021-01-01. When facts arose before that date or span it, check the Supreme People's Court rules on temporal application rather than assuming the Civil Code automatically governs every event.

## Judicial interpretations
For topics with a current Supreme People's Court interpretation, check the latest effective text and amendment status. Especially watch for interpretations concerning:
- temporal application of the Civil Code;
- General Provisions;
- contracts;
- guarantee/security system;
- marriage and family;
- inheritance;
- tort and other subject-specific civil issues.

Do not rely on a remembered interpretation title or article number without verification.

## Cases
Official guiding, typical, reference or gazette cases can help explain application. State why the facts are similar/different. Do not present a case as a statute or assume identical outcomes.
